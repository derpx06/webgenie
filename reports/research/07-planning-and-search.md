# Planning, search and recovery for long-horizon web tasks

Stream 07. It targets the two failure classes that start the longest failures in the 300-task Online-Mind2Web run (`3fdf2fa`): **planner error 30%** (re-issues a dead route, no memory of what failed, answers "none exist" too early) and **wrong route 17%**. It also covers the planning half of **wrong target 11%**. The two numbers behind the whole brief: failures take **3.3×** the steps of successes, and **44 of 64** died at the token cap while going round in circles (`analysis-2-step-waste.md` §4).

It builds on, and does not repeat, the following: 01 §1/§5 (Anthropic's loop guidance, Huang et al. on self-correction, Manus's "keep the wrong stuff in"), 02 §1/§6 and "Three not to copy" (WebPilot 37.2%, MCTS needs resets, AWM, SkillWeaver), and 03 (Kapoor et al.: cost-controlled evaluation, pass^k).

**Model scope.** WebGenie must work with any provider (OpenAI, Anthropic, Gemini, Ollama and others). Every technique below is marked with how much it depends on model strength. Recommendations are ranked higher when they are **harness-side and deterministic**, because those work the same whether the model is strong or weak.

---

## 0. Something the repository shows before any paper

Before looking outward, I checked what the r3 build (`3fdf2fa`) already told the models (`git show 3fdf2fa:…/prompts/templates/planner.ts`, `navigator.ts`):

- The planner prompt already had a **"# Routes that fail"** section: "when the same search, the same page or the same read already failed … do not plan it again in other words. Choose a different route".
- It already said to apply requirements with the site's own sort and filter controls, **"not only by typing them into a search box"**, and it already had the absence rule ("Answer that something does not exist only when … every criterion was applied").
- The navigator already had "click the suggestion that matches instead of pressing Enter" and "if an approach fails twice, change the approach".
- `GoalRepeats` (word-Jaccard ≥ 0.75 over goal text; sent back at 3, forced to finish at 5) was **in** r3.
- The harness-written `TaskLedger` ([ALREADY TRIED]) came **after** r3 (`0f55b94`), as did `notes`, `todo_list` and `assert`.

**So the 30% planner-error and 11% wrong-target failures happened while the correct rule was already in the prompt.** Plan items 3.1–3.4 are written as rules, and a rule the model already had did not stop these failures. The literature below agrees: what works is **state the harness keeps and checks**, not more instructions (MAST: "structural redesigns beyond simple prompt engineering"; Magentic-One's ledgers; AgentOccam's plan tree). Every recommendation in this brief is built that way.

## 1. Planner/executor splits and hierarchical planning

**Practice.** Almost every strong web or computer-use agent separates *what to do next* (a planner, orchestrator or manager) from *how to do it on this page* (an executor or navigator). They differ on three things: what the planner sees, how often it runs, and whether the progress record is kept by the model or by the harness.

**Mechanisms and evidence.**

| System | Planner sees | Replans when | Numbers | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Plan-and-Act** (Erdogan et al.) | query, current HTML, previous plan and actions | **after every executor step** ("dynamic replanning") | WebArena-Lite ablation with a fine-tuned Llama planner: no planner 9.85% → base planner 14.21 → fine-tuned 22.42 → +synthetic data 29.63 → **+dynamic replanning 44.24%** (the largest single step in the table). Final 57.58% WA-Lite, 81.36% WebVoyager | [arXiv 2503.09572](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.09572), Mar 2025, ICML 2025 |
| **Magentic-One** (Microsoft) | a **Task Ledger** (given facts, facts to look up, facts to derive, educated guesses, plan) and a **Progress Ledger** that answers five questions each step: is the request satisfied? **are we looping?** **is progress being made?** who acts next? what instruction? | the inner loop runs until a **stall counter** passes a small threshold (the paper uses 2); then the outer loop reflects, updates facts and guesses, and **rewrites the plan** | GAIA 38.0%, AssistantBench 13.3%, WebArena 32.8%. Ablation: removing the ledgers is reported as a **~31% drop**. Top error classes: *persistent inefficient actions*, *insufficient verification* | [arXiv 2411.04468](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04468), Nov 2024 |
| **Agent S2** (Simular) | earlier subgoals, latest observation, original instruction | **after each subgoal**, whether it succeeded or failed ("proactive hierarchical planning") | proactive vs reactive on a 65-task OSWorld subset: **+4.62 pp at 15 steps, +6.15 pp at 50 steps** | [arXiv 2504.00906](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.00906), Apr 2025 |

| **MAST** (failure taxonomy, 1,642 traces from 7 multi-agent frameworks) | — | — | step repetition **15.7%**, unaware of termination conditions **12.4%**, reasoning–action mismatch 13.2%, incorrect verification 9.1%, incomplete verification 8.2%, premature termination **6.2%**. A better role specification alone: +9.4 pp | [arXiv 2503.13657](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.13657), Mar 2025 |

**What these results share:**

1. **Replanning at subgoal boundaries or on every step beats a fixed plan.** This is the largest ablation effect in Plan-and-Act, and it holds on OSWorld too. The gain in Plan-and-Act was measured with a *fine-tuned* planner. Agent S2's +4.6–6.2 pp used frontier models without fine-tuning, so the direction holds across model strengths, but the size of the gain does not transfer directly.
2. **"Are we looping / are we making progress" is asked explicitly, every step, and answered against a record.** Magentic-One makes the *model* answer. In WebGenie, `stalled`, `GoalRepeats` and `validatedProgress` already compute most of these answers *deterministically*, which is the better choice for weak models (§3).
3. **A failed branch is pruned from the working context and kept as a one-line lesson** (AgentOccam's prune, and Magentic-One's outer loop that "identifies failures, updates facts"). This fits Manus's "keep the wrong stuff in" (01) once you separate the two things: keep the lesson, drop the raw steps.

## 2. Lookahead without resets

**Practice.** There are two families: **tree search** over real states (LATS, Koh et al., ExACT/R-MCTS, WebPilot) and **model-based planning**, where an LLM imagines each candidate action's outcome before acting (WebDreamer, WMA, WebEvolver).

| Method | Mechanism | Numbers | Needs | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Tree search for LM agents** (Koh et al.) | best-first search, value function from a multimodal LLM, b=5, d=5, budget c=20 expansions | VWA 18.9 → **26.4%** (+39.7% rel.); WebArena 15.0 → 19.2%. Budget scaling: c=5 +30.6% rel., c=20 +51.0% rel. (200-task subset) | **backtracking = reset the environment and replay the actions**, because navigating back loses page state. Irreversible actions called out as a blocker for deployment | [arXiv 2407.01476](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01476), Jul 2024 (v4 Feb 2026) |
| **LATS** | MCTS + LM value + self-reflection | WebShop score 75.9 (GPT-3.5) | "an environment … supporting state reversions" | [arXiv 2310.04406](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.04406), Oct 2023 |
| **ExACT / R-MCTS** | MCTS + contrastive reflection + multi-agent-debate value; then distil the search into the model | +6–30% rel. over prior SOTA on VWA; the fine-tuned model recovers **87%** of R-MCTS performance without search | resets for the search; **fine-tuning** for the distillation | [arXiv 2410.02052](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02052), Oct 2024 |
| **WebDreamer** | the LLM simulates each candidate action's outcome in text, then scores it; best horizon **H=1** | VWA reactive 17.6 → 23.6 (tree search 26.2); **Online-Mind2Web 26.0 → 37.0%**; Mind2Web-Live 20.2 → 25.0. Apr 2025) |
| **WMA** (Chae et al.) | a world model predicts a *transition-focused* summary of what changed, and a value model ranks the simulated outcomes | the preliminary study found **LLMs predict next states at ~54.75% on a binary task (near chance)**. Given simulated outcomes, GPT-4o action choice rose **53% → 73%**. WebArena 13.1 → 16.6%. **5.3× faster and 6.8× cheaper than tree search** | a *fine-tuned* world model; prompt-only world models do markedly worse (their ablation) | [arXiv 2410.13232](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13232), Oct 2024, ICLR 2025 |
| **WebEvolver** | a co-evolving world model as the imagination engine at inference time | ~+10% over self-evolving baselines (Mind2Web-Live, WebVoyager, GAIA-web) | training loop | [arXiv 2504.21024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.21024), Apr 2025 |

**What transfers to a live, logged-in tab that cannot be reset:**

- **Search over real states: no, with one exception.** Every search method backtracks by reset and replay, and Koh et al. say plainly that navigating back is not enough. WebGenie's tab carries cookies, carts and submitted forms. The exception is **URL-addressable, read-only states**: a results page whose query and filters are all in the URL *can* be returned to exactly with `go_to_url`. That is a real checkpoint, the one kind of "reset" a live tab offers (recommendation R7).
- **Simulation: the H=1 idea, not the machinery.** WebDreamer's gain comes from picking between candidates using a *predicted outcome*. WMA shows that prompted LLMs are near chance at predicting transitions, so a simulated world model is **strongly model-dependent**: plausible with frontier models, unreliable with small local models. It also costs one model call per candidate, and our provider limit is requests per minute (429s correlate 0.91 with request count; `analysis-3-latency.md`). The part that holds for any model is **looking before committing, by reading the real page instead of imagining it**: does this results page actually have a facet for each pending requirement? The page is available, cheap to read, and never wrong about itself (R5).
- **Branching without harm: background tabs.** A GET link opened in a new tab is a branch you can close. That is the only non-destructive branching a live browser has. It helps only for read-only exploration, the fan-out case 01/02 already allow.

## 3. Reflection and recovery

**Practice → mechanism → evidence.**

- **Reflexion** stores verbal reflections *across trials* and needs a success signal between them. On WebShop it **did not beat ReAct after four trials**, and the authors conclude it "is unable to solve tasks that require a significant amount of diversity and exploration": e-commerce search needs different *routes*, not better introspection ([arXiv 2303.11366](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11366), Appendix B.1, Mar 2023). WebGenie gets one trial per task, so cross-trial reflection does not apply.
- **Self-correction without external feedback does not work.** Huang et al. is covered in 01. Kamoi et al. (TACL 2024): "no prior work demonstrates successful self-correction with feedback from prompted LLMs" except on tasks exceptionally suited to it; it works with *reliable external feedback* or fine-tuning ([arXiv 2406.01297](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01297), Jun 2024). On web tasks the external feedback is free: the page itself, the validation verdict, the ledger.
- **Models condition on their own errors.** Sinha et al.: accuracy falls as the context fills with the model's *own* earlier mistakes, and model scale barely helps. **Thinking models are largely immune** (Qwen3-thinking stays stable at turn 100 whatever the error rate). Horizon: GPT-5 thinking reached 2,176 steps, while non-thinking frontier models fail beyond ~6 steps per turn. They recommend **showing fewer past failures in context**, and found self-verification prompts insufficient ([arXiv 2509.09677](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.09677), Sep 2025, ICLR 2026). This is the mechanism behind "failures recirculate". It is **model-dependent**: it matters most for non-thinking and small models, exactly the ones a provider-agnostic extension must support.
- **Re-checking helps once and hurts when repeated.** TTI's prompting-only study on a WebArena subset: CoT 23.81% → **~28% with one "double-check"**; the agent changed its action ~25% of the time. Repeated re-checks caused confusion and lost accuracy on some domains ([arXiv 2506.07976](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07976), Jun 2025).
- **Learning from failures, not only from successes.** ReasoningBank turns successful *and* failed trajectories into titled strategy items. WebArena with Gemini-2.5-flash: no memory 40.5 → AWM 44.1 → **ReasoningBank 48.8%**, and steps 9.7 → 8.3. On WebArena-Shopping, success-only memories scored 46.5%, and **adding failures raised it to 49.7%** ([arXiv 2509.25140](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25140), Sep 2025, ICLR 2026). This is cross-task memory (Stream 9). The lesson that carries over *within* a task: a failure should be written down as a reusable *reason* ("keyword search on this site has no condition facet"), not as a raw trace.
- **Stall detection is structural in every strong system.** Magentic-One's stall counter (threshold 2) triggers an outer-loop replan. MAST's two largest system-design failures are step repetition (15.7%) and being unaware of termination conditions (12.4%). Online-Mind2Web: most agents other than Operator "are prone to getting stuck at dead ends"; Operator takes nearly 2× steps on failures "due to repeated actions and pop-ups" ([arXiv 2504.01382](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.01382), Apr 2025).
- **Backtracking agents** (BacktrackAgent, EMNLP 2025, [arXiv 2505.20660](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2505.20660)) train a verifier, judger and reflector to detect a wrong step from the *outcome page* and undo it. The gains are on mobile GUI benchmarks and need training, so there are no transferable numbers. The idea that carries over: judge a step by the page it produced (which WebGenie's `validation/` already does), then return to the last good state (R7).

**When reflection helps and when it hurts.** It helps when it is **anchored on external evidence** (a page, a validation verdict, a count of results), **happens once at the right moment** (a stall, a sent-back plan), and **produces a structural change** (a new route kind, a pruned branch). It hurts when it is **intrinsic** (re-reading its own reasoning), **repeated**, or when it **leaves the failed transcript in context** for a model that self-conditions.

## 4. Route selection: site search, filters, URLs, menus

**Evidence.**

- **Filters are where agents fail most.** In Online-Mind2Web, "applying incorrect filters or sorting options, or failing to apply them when required" accounts for **57.7% of Operator's failures**. Numeric ranges get applied loosely ("2001 to 2012" for "2004 to 2012"), and the paper names "excessive reliance on keyword search" and weak discovery of niche site features as causes. A naive search-engine agent scores 51% on WebVoyager but **22%** on Online-Mind2Web, so shortcut routes do not carry this benchmark ([arXiv 2504.01382](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.01382)). This is exactly our wrong-target class ("without nuts" typed into the query instead of the Exclude filter).
- **The site's own functions beat UI improvisation.** WALT reverse-engineers the functions a site already provides (search, filter, sort, often as **URL parameters**) into tools. WebArena 50.1% (prior best 40.4); VWA 52.9%. Removing the tools costs **7–30 pp**, and they cut steps by 27%. "Find the cheapest blue kayak" collapses to `search(query='blue kayak', category='Boats', sort_by='price')` ([arXiv 2510.01524](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.01524v1), Oct 2025). What carries over without training: **a route is a (query, facets, sort) triple, and on many sites it is visible in the URL**, so it can be read, recorded and rebuilt. SkillWeaver and API-hybrid results are covered in 02.
- **Query formulation: short and broad first, then narrow.** Anthropic's research system saw agents write "overly specific, lengthy search queries that yielded minimal results", "search endlessly for nonexistent sources" and "continue … after gathering sufficient information". The fix was prompt guidance to "start with short, broad queries … then progressively narrow" ([Anthropic, 13 Jun 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)). Reflexion's WebShop appendix names the same trap: e-commerce search engines handle ambiguous natural-language queries badly. For a site search box this is the rule: **item name in the query, criteria in the facets.**
- **Absence answers: inviting them is expensive, and so is never allowing them.** In WebArena, adding the instruction that unachievable tasks should be answered "N/A" made GPT-4 label **54.9% of *feasible* tasks as impossible**, and overall success *fell* 14.41 → 11.70% ([arXiv 2307.13854](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.13854), Jul 2023). The opposite failure appears in Anthropic's "searching endlessly for nonexistent sources". BrowseComp shows persistence pays on genuinely hard lookups (Deep Research 51.5% vs GPT-4o-with-browsing 1.9%), but browsing models are badly calibrated (Deep Research calibration error **91%**) ([arXiv 2504.12516](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.12516), Apr 2025). So the model's own "I'm sure it's not there" cannot decide it. The literature offers no fixed "try N routes" number. The defensible rule is structural: **an absence answer needs evidence from at least two *different kinds* of route** (for example, site search by the item's own name *and* the category/facet path, or the site plus web search), and after that it is allowed, not refused again.

## 5. Budgets: when to stop, re-plan or ask

- **Spend extra compute on interaction, not on samples.** TTI, at equal token budgets: **interaction scaling gives the steepest curve**, budget-forced longer thinking plateaus near 26%, and best-of-n (n=3,5,7) adds **<3 pp** at the highest cost. Trained with a curriculum over the horizon, Gemma-3-12B goes 55.8 → **64.8%** on WebVoyager and 18.3 → 26.1% on WebArena. Fixed h=30 training produced "aimless exploration" and did *worse* than h=10 ([arXiv 2506.07976](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07976)). More steps help only when they are *exploratory*. WebGenie's failures already use 3.3× the steps, spent recirculating, so the budget goes to *new routes*, not more of the same.
- **Spend per-step compute only where the decision is contested.** CATTS samples N candidate actions per step and uses vote entropy and margin to call an arbiter only when uncertain. On WebArena-Lite: ReAct 38.8% at 96k tokens, majority vote at N=10 **43.2% at 920k**, **CATTS 47.9% at 745k**. When the arbiter *overrode* a strong consensus (margin > 0.7), tasks succeeded only **35.0% vs 46.9%** (p=0.026) ([arXiv 2602.12276](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.12276v1), Feb 2026, GPT-OSS-120B). The lesson for any model: extra deliberation is worth buying at genuinely uncertain points, and harmful when it second-guesses a clear decision.
- **Best-of-N over whole trajectories needs resettable environments.** Agent S3's Behavior Best-of-N: a single rollout scores 62.6% on OSWorld, N=10 scores 69.9%, a mixed-model ensemble **72.6%** (human 72.36%), pass@N 80.5%. The paper assumes "multiple independent rollouts from the same initial state" on parallel VMs ([arXiv 2510.02250](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.02250), Oct 2025). BrowseComp's best-of-64 is read-only browsing. Neither fits a user's logged-in tab.
- **Agents need to know their budget.** "Simply increasing the tool-call budget fails to improve performance" because agents are not aware of their budget. A **Budget Tracker** that shows the remaining budget, and BATS, which uses it to choose between digging deeper and pivoting, give better scaling curves ([arXiv 2511.17006](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.17006), Nov 2025, COLM 2026; *per-benchmark numbers not retrieved*). Anthropic: token usage explains **80%** of BrowseComp variance (95% together with tool calls and model choice), and effort-scaling rules sit in the prompt: simple lookups get 3–10 tool calls ([Anthropic, Jun 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)).
- **Hand off to a human after a failure threshold.** The OpenAI guide lists two triggers: "exceeding failure thresholds" and high-risk actions. Magentic-UI on GAIA: autonomous 30.3% → **42.6%** with a simulated user asked in 4.3% of tasks → **51.9%** when that user had side information, asked in 10% of tasks ([arXiv 2507.22358](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.22358), Jul 2025). Within WebGenie's HITL policy (ask only what the user alone can settle), this supports one question *at a stall caused by a user-resolvable ambiguity*, not as a fallback for any failure.

## What this means for WebGenie

The code as it stands (post-r3): `executor.ts` replans on the triggers in `contracts/replan.ts` (initial, human, sent back, navigator done or error, 2 unvalidated steps, a stall of 3 identical of the last 6, or the interval of 5, doubled while on track). `GoalRepeats` keys on **goal words** (Jaccard ≥ 0.75). `TaskLedger` (`memory/in-chat/ledger.ts`) records **addresses** (visits), **refusals** and **dead ends only on `go_back`**, and `ContextBuilder.stateSections` already shows it to **both** roles as `[ALREADY TRIED]`. So plan item 3.1 ("surface the ledger to the planner") is already shipped, and was never measured, because it postdates r3. The models see "Current step N/100", but the binding limit in the runs was the token cap. Failures die at a median of 33 steps and 505k tokens, so "33/100" tells the planner it has plenty left.

| **R9** | **Replan at subgoal boundaries.** Add a trigger: the current plan's `success_condition` is met (validated step + evidence) → replan (Agent S2). Keep the interval as a safety net. **Do not ship plan item 3.5 (interval 5 → 7) on its own.** The strongest replanning ablation in the literature (Plan-and-Act, 29.6 → 44.2) says *more* replanning at the right moments, and 3.5 was justified on seconds, not accuracy. | `contracts/replan.ts`, `executor.ts` | planner error, wrong route | fewer steps on a finished subgoal; timely route changes. It may *add* planner calls, which costs RPM (Stream 7) | direction holds across models (S2 used frontier models); size varies | **gated**, together with 3.5 |
| **R10** | **A binding budget the planner can see, with pivot points.** Show `budget used: X% (steps/tokens, whichever binds)`. At ~50% with no progress from any route (R3) → the planner must switch *route kind*. At ~80% → finish with the best answer and what was tried, or ask the user if the blocker is theirs to settle. Also give the product a real per-task budget; it has none today ("There is no input token budget", `docs/agent.md`). | `prompts/base.ts` (step info), `executor.ts`, `types.ts` options | the 44 cap deaths | turns cap deaths into answers of what was found and tried (BATS, TTI, Anthropic's effort scaling). Risk: finishing too early. That is why it is gated and why the thresholds are options | none | **gated** |
| **R11** | **Adaptive reasoning effort for the planner.** Raise the planner's reasoning effort *only* on replans triggered by a stall, a sent-back plan or a repeated route, on providers that support it (`llmCapabilities.ts`); keep the normal effort otherwise. This is CATTS's "compute where contested" without N× requests, and Sinha's finding that thinking breaks self-conditioning. | `agent/helper.ts`, `agents/planner.ts` | planner error at the moments it happens | better decisions at the ~5 contested points per failing task, for little extra cost | **strongly model-dependent**: no-op on models without reasoning control | **gated** |

**Priority.** R1, R2, R3, R6 and R7 are deterministic, cheap and testable. They belong in Wave 1 and replace plan items 3.1–3.4 as written, which are prompt rules r3 already had. R4 ships with them but its effect is read off the next gate run. R5 (behaviour), R8, R9 + 3.5, R10 and R11 change *when the agent stops or what it sees*. Under the accuracy-first rule they go into one gate run, in Wave 2 next to 1.1/1.2. Every one of them is measured by the same trace scripts: the planner-error and wrong-route first-divergence shares, cap deaths, and steps per failure.

**What to measure (not guess) before the gate:** how often R2's route key and today's goal-word key disagree on the r3 traces, and how many of the 19 planner-error tasks show a route re-issued with *different* goal wording. That count predicts R2's value.

## What not to copy

| Technique | Why not here |
|---|---|
| **Tree search / MCTS on the live tab** (Koh, LATS, ExACT, WebPilot) | backtracking is reset + replay, which a logged-in tab cannot do safely. ~3× the steps and 749 s vs 184 s per task. Every rollout multiplies requests against an RPM-limited provider (37% of r3 requests already failed). The part worth keeping, URL-addressable checkpoints, is R7. |
| **LLM world-model simulation** (WebDreamer, WMA, WebEvolver) | prompted LLMs predict transitions near chance (54.75%); the gains come from GPT-4o-class or specially *trained* world models, and H > 1 degrades. One extra call per candidate. Strongly model-dependent, which conflicts with provider-agnosticism. Reading the real page (R5) gives the same "look before you commit" without guessing. |
| **Best-of-N over trajectories** (Agent S3 bBoN, BrowseComp best-of-64) | needs independent environments from the same start state; running it on a user's tab repeats real side effects. |
| **Per-step voting with an arbiter** (CATTS) | N× requests per step under an RPM limit, and an arbiter that overrides a strong consensus loses 11.9 pp. The part worth keeping, extra deliberation only when contested, is R11. |
| **Reflexion-style cross-trial reflection** | needs retries and an external success signal between them. No gain on WebShop, the task family closest to ours. |
| **A self-critique or "double-check everything" step** | Huang, Kamoi: no gain without external feedback. TTI: repeated re-checks confuse the agent. (Already dropped in the plan; this adds the TTI number.) |
| **More prompt rules for the same failure classes** | r3 already had the filter, route-switch, suggestion and absence rules and still failed on them. Prompt text also *costs* accuracy when it invites early exits (WebArena's N/A hint: 14.41 → 11.70%). |
| **Training-based gains** (Plan-and-Act synthetic plans, TTI RL, ExACT distillation, Dreamer-7B) | the numbers depend on fine-tuning a specific model; WebGenie ships prompts to whatever provider the user configures. Use the *mechanisms* (replanning, a horizon curriculum, route memory); do not quote their numbers as expectations. |
| **A planner blind to the page** (Agent-E) | its own authors report over-decomposition. WebGenie's planner seeing the state is correct and should stay. |
| **Leaderboard claims as targets** | e.g. WebVoyager, where a naive search agent reaches 51% but only 22% on Online-Mind2Web. |
